<section class="section" id="overview">
  <div class="wrap section-grid"><div><p class="eyebrow">Our approach</p><h2>Task-agnostic control and user-specific assistance.</h2></div><div><p class="large-copy">Conventional control methodologies for lower-limb exoskeletons often rely on pre-defined reference kinematics or task-specific torque profiles. These approaches can constrain voluntary motion and require parameter adjustment across users and locomotor tasks.</p><p>We investigate reference kinematics-free control strategies based on energy shaping, centroidal momentum regulation, and control barrier functions. Related research addresses length-actuated prostheses, continuum robotic systems, and passivity-based bipedal locomotion. Mathematical analysis, simulation, and human-subject experiments are used to characterize controller performance and human–robot interaction.</p><div class="research-principles"><div><h3>Control and Customization</h3><p>Develop task-agnostic control strategies and customize robotic assistance through human-in-the-loop optimization.</p></div><div><h3>Design</h3><p>Develop and prototype user-friendly wearable devices and compliant mechanisms for physical interaction with human users.</p></div><div><h3>Evaluations</h3><p>Conduct biomechanics studies to characterize human movement and evaluate assistance through muscle activity, joint mechanics, and stability-related outcomes.</p></div></div></div></div>
</section>
<section class="research-program" id="control-customization"><header class="wrap program-heading"><p class="eyebrow">01 / Control and Customization</p><h2>Task-Agnostic Control and Customization</h2><p>Control formulations and optimization frameworks for assistance that accommodates user-selected movement.</p></header>
<section class="section research-topic shaded" id="exoskeletons">
  <div class="wrap">
    <p class="eyebrow">Control formulation · Energy and momentum</p><h3 class="project-title">Task-agnostic assistance through energy and momentum shaping</h3>
    <p class="topic-lede">Task-specific reference trajectories can restrict the adaptability of lower-limb exoskeletons to continuously varying human activities. We investigate control paradigms that regulate human body energetics and centroidal momentum without prescribing reference joint kinematics.</p>
    <div class="project-narrative">
      <figure class="topic-figure ral-platform"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/ral-2026-hip-exoskeleton.png" width="1260" height="636" alt="SportsMate 5 hip exoskeleton and participant wearing hip actuators, IMUs, and a Raspberry Pi" loading="lazy"><figcaption>SportsMate 5 hip exoskeleton and experimental instrumentation. Figure 1 from Yu, Dean &amp; Lv, <a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/files/RAL26_Final.pdf">IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, 2026</a>.</figcaption></figure>
      <h4>Control objective</h4><p>Kinematic control approaches depend on pre-defined reference trajectories that can be difficult to adjust across locomotor tasks and human subjects. For individuals with partial or full voluntary control of their lower limbs, such references may overly constrain self-selected motion.</p>
      <h4>Energy and momentum formulations</h4><p>Energy shaping modifies the closed-loop mechanical energy of the coupled human–exoskeleton system. Potential energy shaping can provide body-weight support, whereas kinetic energy shaping modifies effective mass and inertia. Highly backdrivable actuation facilitates voluntary movement while enabling the commanded assistive torques.</p><p>Our centroidal momentum shaping paradigm regulates the sum of limb momenta projected onto the human center of mass. A virtual reference model is constructed from each user’s real-time gait with scaled limb inertial parameters. A nonlinear disturbance observer estimates voluntary human joint torques, and these estimates are incorporated into the control design.</p>
      <div class="evidence-note"><h4>Hip-exoskeleton validation</h4><p>Our 2026 IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters study reports preliminary experiments with six non-disabled participants wearing a bilateral hip exoskeleton during treadmill walking, ground–stair transitions, and sit-to-stand tasks. Ensemble-averaged muscle efforts decreased by 22% with assistance. These findings are specific to the preliminary study conditions.</p></div>

<section class="section research-topic" id="personalization">
  <div class="wrap"><p class="eyebrow">Control customization · Human-in-the-loop optimization</p><h3 class="project-title">Human-in-the-loop customization of assistance</h3><p class="topic-lede">The relationship between control parameters and human effort varies across individuals and locomotor activities. We combine task-agnostic assistive strategies with sample-efficient optimization to customize lower-limb exoskeleton assistance.</p>
    <div class="topic-grid text-pair"><div><h4>Customization objective</h4><p>Although energy-shaping strategies can provide task-agnostic assistance, their parameters influence the resulting human joint torques and energetic demands. A systematic customization framework is needed to identify appropriate parameters across users and tasks.</p><h4>Optimization framework</h4><p>We investigate derivative-free, sample-efficient optimization in conjunction with energy-shaping control. The framework searches for assistive parameters using human performance-based objectives while retaining a task-agnostic controller structure.</p></div><div><div class="method-panel"><p class="eyebrow">The customization loop</p><ol><li><strong>Set the assistance.</strong> Choose parameters within an energy-shaping strategy.</li><li><strong>Evaluate the response.</strong> Assess the resulting movement and effort objective.</li><li><strong>Update the parameters.</strong> Use optimization to search for improved assistance.</li></ol></div><div class="evidence-note"><h4>Simulation and computational studies</h4><p>Simulation studies of the learning framework demonstrate reductions in modeled human joint torque and metabolic cost. Related work investigates a two-layer human-in-the-loop optimization structure and physics-informed neural networks for solving the energy-shaping matching condition. Simulation findings are distinguished from experimental validation.</p></div></div></div>

<section class="section research-topic shaded" id="safety">
  <div class="wrap"><p class="eyebrow">Stability augmentation · Perturbed locomotion</p><h3 class="project-title">Human-centered stability augmentation control</h3><p class="topic-lede">The vast majority of lower-limb exoskeleton controllers are developed and validated under steady-state locomotor conditions. We investigate proactive stability augmentation under leaning and slip perturbations while preserving voluntary human motion.</p>
    <div class="topic-grid text-pair"><div><h4>Stability objective</h4><p>Control paradigms validated under nominal walking may not generalize to unstable conditions or external perturbations. In addition, task-specific recovery trajectories can constrain the user’s voluntary responses.</p><h4>Control Barrier Function formulation</h4><p>The proposed paradigm defines a center of mass-based stability indicator and enforces its dynamics within a user-specific safe range through control barrier functions. Corrective assistance is generated as the system state approaches the safety-set boundary. A nonlinear disturbance observer estimates a lumped input comprising voluntary human joint torque and external perturbations; this estimate is incorporated into the control design.</p></div><div><div class="method-panel"><p class="eyebrow">The research question</p><p class="large-copy">Proactive stability assistance without pre-defined reference kinematics.</p><p>The controller accounts for user-specific stability constraints, voluntary joint torques, and external perturbations within a common control formulation.</p></div><div class="evidence-note"><h4>Leaning and slip experiments</h4><p>The final Humanoids 2026 manuscript reports preliminary experiments with two non-disabled participants wearing a bilateral hip exoskeleton. Ensemble-averaged muscle efforts decreased by 41% during leaning and 12% during slip perturbations, with lower peak-to-peak whole-body angular momentum and faster recovery. These preliminary results do not establish efficacy outside the tested participants and conditions.</p></div></div></div>
    <div class="key-papers"><h4>Related ARL publications</h4><ul>

<section class="section research-topic" id="prosthetics">
  <div class="wrap"><p class="eyebrow">Device development · Prosthetic systems</p><h3 class="project-title">Length-actuated transfemoral prostheses</h3><p class="topic-lede">We investigate dynamic leg-length actuation as an alternative prosthetic design methodology. The objective is to coordinate stance support and swing-phase clearance through active shortening and lengthening of a transfemoral prosthesis.</p>
    <div class="topic-grid"><div><h4>Design requirements</h4><p>Transfemoral prosthesis users may experience challenges in stance stability, push-off, and swing-phase toe clearance. These requirements motivate prosthetic mechanisms that actively modify leg length during ambulation.</p><h4>Length-actuation mechanism</h4><p>The proposed device employs a prismatic mechanism to modify leg length during the gait cycle. Dynamic leg-length modeling informs the desired actuation profile, and experimental evaluation incorporates user preferences and gait biomechanics.</p><div class="evidence-note"><h4>Gait evaluation</h4><p>Experiments with participants with amputation investigate gait symmetry, stability, and mobility with the length-actuated device. The design and preliminary evaluation are reported in IEEE Access (2024), with a subsequent case study in Prosthetics and Orthotics International (2026).</p></div></div><figure class="topic-figure prosthesis-figure"><div class="prosthesis-images"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Amputee.png" alt="A participant wearing the length-actuated transfemoral prosthesis" loading="lazy"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/MachineDesign.png" alt="Mechanical design of the length-actuated prosthesis" loading="lazy"></div><figcaption>The length-actuated prosthesis and its mechanical design. <section class="section research-topic shaded" id="continuum">
  <div class="wrap"><p class="eyebrow">Compliant mechanisms · Continuum robotics</p><h3 class="project-title">Compliant robots for physical interaction</h3><p class="topic-lede">We investigate continuum and hybrid rigid–compliant robotic systems for physical interaction with human users and unstructured environments. Structural compliance provides a means of accommodating contact and geometric variation during assistance and manipulation.</p>
    <div class="topic-grid"><div><h4>Wearable assistance and fall mitigation</h4><p>Continuum robots achieve distributed bending through compliant structures. We investigate their use as auxiliary limbs and wearable appendages for daily assistance, including the preliminary design and evaluation of a continuum robot-based fall-mitigation system.</p><p>Related work explores a continuum-legged biped with a kangaroo-inspired tail for tripedal locomotion, extending compliant mechanisms to new robot architectures.</p><h4>Hybrid rigid–continuum grasping</h4><p>In collaboration with Prof. Ian Walker’s group, we investigate an avian-inspired gripper that integrates actuated rigid structures with compliant continuum digits. The hybrid architecture combines load-bearing capability with adaptability during grasping. Related work develops a teleoperation framework for the compliant gripper.</p></div><div class="topic-gallery"><figure class="topic-figure"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/ClawFigure.png" alt="The Claw prototype with three compliant forward digits and an opposing digit" loading="lazy"><figcaption>The Claw combines rigid elements with compliant continuum digits for adaptable grasping.</figcaption></figure><figure class="topic-figure"><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Backward%20Fall.png" alt="A sequence showing a mannequin and wearable continuum robot during a fall-mitigation experiment" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Preliminary evaluation of a continuum-robot wearable system for fall mitigation.</figcaption></figure></div></div>

<section class="section research-foundations" id="foundations">
  <div class="wrap section-grid"><div><p class="eyebrow">Control foundations</p><h2>Robust bipedal locomotion</h2></div><div><p class="large-copy">Passivity-based control provides a foundation for reasoning about energy, stability, and interaction in legged systems.</p><p>Our bipedal locomotion work introduces a generalized energy storage function that accounts for energy stored and dissipated by an inner-loop controller. An outer-loop passivity-based controller is designed to enlarge the basin of attraction, improve robustness to ground slope, and accelerate convergence to a stable walking cycle.</p><p><a href="{{ site.baseurl }}/files/GeASME.pdf">Decentralized Passivity-Based Control with a Generalized Energy Storage Function for Robust Biped Locomotion</a><br><span class="paper-meta">Yeatman, Lv &amp; Gregg · ASME Journal of Dynamic Systems, Measurement, and Control · 2019</span></p><a class="text-link" href="{{ site.baseurl }}/publication/Passivity">Explore the foundational work →</a></div></div>
